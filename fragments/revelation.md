# The Book of Revelation

The falling cities can be read beside Revelation 18, where Babylon, the great city of wealth and commerce, falls and becomes an object of lament. This is a biblical resonance, rather than a source identified in Eliot's printed notes. His note to this part of the poem instead explicitly mentions the contemporary decay of eastern Europe.

Jerusalem, Athens, Alexandria, Vienna, and London collapse into one sequence. Sacred, classical, and modern cities lose their separate historical distances. The repeated “Unreal” makes their monuments appear unstable even before they fall: the reader encounters civilization as overlapping ruins rather than a secure inheritance.
