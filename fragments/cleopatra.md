# Antony and Cleopatra

The opening of “A Game of Chess” recasts Enobarbus's description of Cleopatra's barge in Shakespeare's *Antony and Cleopatra*, Act II, scene ii. Shakespeare's burnished throne becomes the chair in a modern interior. The echoes continue through the Cupidon figures, brilliant surfaces, perfume, and overwhelming display.

The borrowed magnificence gives an unnamed woman the setting of a legendary queen, but the room's sensual abundance leads into strained, repetitive conversation. The allusion allows glamour and distress to occupy the same space. It does not establish that the woman is simply Cleopatra in modern dress; the older scene is one fragment among several crowding her room.
