# Lil and Albert

Lil's story reaches us through a companion's pub conversation rather than through direct access to Lil herself. Albert returns from military service expecting pleasure; Lil has lost her teeth, borne five children, and taken pills to end a pregnancy. The companion treats these bodily costs as failures to keep a husband satisfied.

The scene gives the poem's exhausted fertility an economic and domestic form. Reproduction has produced injury rather than renewal, while the threat of another woman turns friendship into surveillance. The narrator's confident judgments should not be mistaken for the poem's own verdict. Lil's brief replies expose pressures that the surrounding chatter tries to make ordinary.
