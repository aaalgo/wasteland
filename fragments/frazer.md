# James Frazer: The Golden Bough

In his introductory notes Eliot acknowledges James George Frazer's *The Golden Bough*, especially its *Adonis, Attis, Osiris* volumes, as a source for the poem's vegetation ceremonies. Frazer brought together accounts of ritual death, mourning, fertility, and returning life within a comparative account of religion. His framework is part of Eliot's source material, rather than an unquestioned account of how all religions developed.

The corpse planted in a garden makes the expectation of returning vegetation grotesquely literal. Phlebas's movement through the stages of age and youth likewise invites a cyclical reading, although that passage carries no specific Frazer note. The poem repeatedly offers the forms of renewal while leaving uncertain whether a dead person or damaged society can actually return.
