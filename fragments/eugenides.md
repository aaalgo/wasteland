# Mr. Eugenides, the Smyrna Merchant

Mr. Eugenides arrives with the vocabulary of international trade: currants from Smyrna, “C.i.f. London,” and “documents at sight.” Eliot's note explains the arrangement for shipment and payment; the source edition also carries an editorial correction expanding c.i.f. as “cost, insurance and freight.” Commercial paperwork briefly becomes the poem's language.

The invitation moves from a City hotel luncheon to a weekend at the Metropole, leaving the relation between business and personal solicitation suggestive rather than explicit. In his note on Tiresias, Eliot says the one-eyed merchant merges with the Phoenician Sailor. Eugenides therefore participates in a recurring sea-trade figure whose modern transactions coexist with older patterns of desire and death.
