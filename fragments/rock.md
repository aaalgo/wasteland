# Rock and No Water

Rock remains where water is wanted: hard, dry matter offers neither drink nor the sound that might promise relief. In “The Burial of the Dead,” the dry stone gives no sound of water. “What the Thunder Said” expands that absence into a mountain landscape where walking, resting, and thinking become difficult. The mouth of the mountain cannot spit; even thunder produces no rain.

The repeated conditional “If there were” makes an imagined spring briefly audible. The lines move from wanting water to wanting only its sound, until the hermit-thrush supplies a deceptive “Drip drop.” The final assertion, “But there is no water,” breaks that imagined relief. Bodily thirst becomes a way of experiencing a world unable to answer desire, while the later expectation of rain keeps renewal possible without yet delivering it.
