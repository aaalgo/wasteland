# Nerval's The Disinherited

Eliot identifies the quoted French line as coming from Gérard de Nerval's sonnet *El Desdichado*, commonly rendered *The Disinherited*. The image is of a prince of Aquitaine whose tower has been destroyed. In Nerval's poem, the speaker's identity is assembled through bereavement, legendary names, and uncertain acts of recognition.

Near the end of *The Waste Land*, the line returns the falling towers of Part V in the form of a damaged personal inheritance. A title and a territory survive in language after the structure that secured them has gone. Its placement beside “my ruins” makes the distinction between cultural collapse and the speaker's own loss difficult to maintain.
