# Ophelia's Farewell

The final farewell in “A Game of Chess” comes from Ophelia in Shakespeare's *Hamlet*, Act IV, scene v. Her broken songs and departures follow her father's death and the collapse of the relationships around her. Her subsequent drowning makes the farewell retrospectively ominous.

Eliot lets ordinary pub goodnights slide into Ophelia's words without announcing a new speaker. Lil's suffering and the chatter about her are suddenly heard through another woman's distress. The quotation does not turn Lil into a literal Ophelia; it lets an inherited theatrical voice carry an anguish the pub conversation has scarcely acknowledged.
