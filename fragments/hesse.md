# Hermann Hesse and the Collapse of Europe

Eliot's note to the falling cities in Part V cites Hermann Hesse's *Blick ins Chaos* (*A Glimpse into Chaos*). The passage describes much of eastern Europe moving toward chaos, singing with the intoxicated fervour of Dostoevsky's Dmitri Karamazov. The respectable citizen laughs; the saint and visionary listen in tears.

The poem converts that diagnosis into an apocalyptic procession: maternal lamentation, wandering crowds, and towers that repeatedly fall. Jerusalem, Athens, Alexandria, Vienna, and London occupy one unstable landscape. Read through Hesse, the catastrophe threatens an inherited European consciousness as well as individual buildings; the succession of cities remains Eliot's imaginative construction, rather than a report of one historical event.
