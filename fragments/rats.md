# Rats' Alley

Rats move between the imagined alley of dead men in “A Game of Chess” and the banks and garret of “The Fire Sermon.” The later passage explicitly recalls the earlier one through Eliot's note to line 115. These are recurrent images within the poem, rather than a single securely identified quotation.

Their soft movements expose what human conversation and the modern city conceal: bodies reduced to bones, waste, and abandoned places. The rat's passage links an anxious private room with the polluted river. Read against the poem's promises of burial and renewal, these remains suggest decay continuing without any assured resurrection.
