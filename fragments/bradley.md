# F. H. Bradley's Appearance and Reality

Eliot's note quotes F. H. Bradley's *Appearance and Reality* on the privacy of experience: each person's sensations, thoughts, and feelings appear within a circle inaccessible to other people. Eliot places this passage beside Dante's prison tower as a source for “each in his prison.”

The image gives the command “Dayadhvam”—sympathize or show compassion—a particular difficulty. Thinking about the key may merely strengthen the thought of confinement. This is the poem's use of Bradley's passage, rather than a summary of his entire philosophy: an account of private experience becomes the felt problem of reaching another person.
