# Pia in Dante's Purgatory

Eliot's note cites Pia's speech in Dante's *Purgatorio*, Canto V. She compresses her earthly history into the places that made and unmade her: Siena and the Maremma. Her brief request to be remembered gives a remarkably small space to a life ended by violence.

The Thames-daughter similarly names Highbury as her birthplace and Richmond and Kew as the places that “undid” her. London's geography takes the grammatical place of Dante's Italian landscape. The echo gives her sexual history the gravity of a damaged life asking to be heard, without supplying a full narrative or making her fate identical to Pia's.
