# Tiresias, the Blind Seer

In Ovid's *Metamorphoses*, Book III, Tiresias has lived as both man and woman. Asked to judge a dispute between Jupiter and Juno about sexual pleasure, he answers Jupiter; Juno blinds him, and Jupiter compensates him with prophetic knowledge. Eliot quotes this episode in his note.

Eliot calls Tiresias the poem's most important personage and says the sexes meet in him. Watching the typist and clerk, the seer carries ancient knowledge into an ordinary modern room. His foresight provides no rescue: the encounter repeats what he has already suffered. The note's claim that he unites the figures offers a connection among their fragments without making their experiences identical.
