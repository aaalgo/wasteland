# Fear in a Handful of Dust

A handful of dust compresses the landscape's barrenness into something that can be held. It also recalls the biblical identification of human life with earth: in Genesis 3:19, the human being is dust and returns to dust. The burial service's language of earth, ashes, and dust belongs to the same field of mortality.

Eliot does not attach a source note to this phrase. Its force need not depend on one exclusive quotation: the offered handful makes an enormous fear small and tangible. Set after the morning and evening shadows, it turns attention from a person's changing outline to the material remainder of the body.
