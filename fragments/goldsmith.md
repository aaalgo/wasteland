# Goldsmith's The Vicar of Wakefield

“When lovely woman stoops to folly” begins the song associated with Olivia in Oliver Goldsmith's *The Vicar of Wakefield*. The song treats a betrayed woman's death as the means to conceal shame and make her lover repent. Its moral and emotional economy makes female suffering answer for male betrayal. See [Goldsmith's song](https://rpo.library.utoronto.ca/content/when-lovely-woman-stoops-folly).

Eliot interrupts the borrowed opening with the typist's ordinary movements: pacing, smoothing her hair, putting on a record. Goldsmith's prescribed tragic ending gives way to automatic continuation. The contrast does not establish that the encounter has left no injury; it shows how little the typist's experience fits a ready-made literary script of remorse and death.
