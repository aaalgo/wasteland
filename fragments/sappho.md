# Sappho and the Returning Sailor

Eliot's note to line 221 names Sappho as a remembered source for evening's power to bring scattered beings home. The surviving fragment commonly numbered 104a addresses the evening star, which brings back what dawn dispersed: sheep, goats, and a child to its mother.

Eliot says that his own sailor is a coastal or dory fisherman returning at nightfall. The ancient pattern of homecoming now leads into the typist's room. Evening still gathers people home, but the encounter that follows offers little of the tenderness or reunion the inherited song might promise.
