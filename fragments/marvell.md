# Marvell's To His Coy Mistress

Eliot's note identifies Andrew Marvell's *To His Coy Mistress*. Marvell's lover hears time's winged chariot approaching and uses the prospect of death to press for immediate erotic fulfilment. Eliot twice reworks the phrase that introduces that pursuing sound.

Here the sounds are rattling bones and then horns and motors bringing Sweeney to Mrs. Porter. Seduction's elegant argument breaks into decay, traffic, and popular song. The borrowed rhythm keeps mortality close to sexual pursuit, while the changing noises make the old poem sound as though it has been overheard and imperfectly reassembled in the modern city.
