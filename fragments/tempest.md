# Shakespeare's The Tempest

Shakespeare's *The Tempest*, Act I, scene ii, supplies several returning phrases. Ariel sings to Ferdinand that his drowned father's eyes have become pearls; Ferdinand hears mysterious music over the water and mourns the king. The supposed death is deceptive within the play, where Alonso is still alive.

Eliot detaches these phrases and redistributes them among a drowned sailor, an anxious room, and the Thames. Sea-change can suggest beautiful transformation, but it also makes a body into precious objects. Because the original scene contains mistaken mourning, its return complicates any simple division between death, survival, and renewal.
