# Shackleton and the Unseen Companion

In *South*, Ernest Shackleton describes the exhausting crossing of South Georgia with Worsley and Crean. Each sensed another presence accompanying the three men. Eliot's note remembers an Antarctic expedition, tentatively identifies Shackleton, and makes this account the stimulus for the unexplained companion in Part V. [Shackleton's account](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/5199/pg5199-images.html) places the experience in the South Georgia march.

Eliot changes the group into two visible walkers and an elusive third. The figure also overlaps the journey to Emmaus, explicitly named among this section's themes. Exhaustion, altered perception, and the possibility of a sacred companion remain suspended together; the poem does not settle the figure's identity.
