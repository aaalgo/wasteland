# The Vigil of Venus

Eliot's note identifies the *Pervigilium Veneris*, an anonymous Latin poem celebrating Venus and the renewal of love in spring. Its ending turns away from the communal festival toward a speaker who cannot sing. The question Eliot borrows asks when the speaker will become like a swallow, and belongs to a longing to end silence.

The swallow returns the bird transformations and wounded voices associated with Philomela, a connection Eliot explicitly asks readers to make. It also carries the closing sequence into Tennyson's swallow song. The reading text's “ceu” is preserved here; the source edition itself records “uti” as an editorial variant rather than silently replacing Eliot's wording.
