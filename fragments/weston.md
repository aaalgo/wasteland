# Jessie Weston: From Ritual to Romance

In his opening note, Eliot acknowledges Jessie L. Weston's *From Ritual to Romance* as a source for the poem's title, plan, and much of its incidental symbolism. Weston connects medieval Grail narratives with fertility ritual, stressing relations among an injured ruler, an afflicted land, and a quest for restoration.

Her account helps connect the Fisher King, fishing, drought, the dangerous chapel, and the final effort to put one's lands in order. It is a source Eliot used, rather than an established explanation of all Grail traditions or a master key to every passage. In the poem, the promised restoration remains surrounded by competing voices and incomplete acts.
