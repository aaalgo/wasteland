# Wagner's Rhine Maidens

Eliot's note calls this passage the song of three Thames-daughters and points to the Rhine-daughters in Act III of Wagner's *Götterdämmerung*. The river spirits of Wagner's drama belong to the story of the stolen Rhine gold; their musical cries return in Eliot's “Weialala” refrain.

The Thames carries oil, tar, barges, and memories of Elizabeth and Leicester. Then three women speak in turn, locating themselves through London places and damaged sexual encounters. Mythic river voices become local human voices. The refrain joins them without repairing the difference between the river's legendary past and its present traffic.
