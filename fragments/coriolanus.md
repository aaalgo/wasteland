# Shakespeare's Coriolanus

Coriolanus is Shakespeare's Roman soldier whose pride and estrangement drive him out of Rome and into alliance with its enemies. His mother's appeal eventually turns him from attacking his own city, at the cost of his position among the Volscians and ultimately his life.

Eliot gives only “a broken Coriolanus,” briefly revived by rumours at nightfall. Set within the meditation on sympathy and private prisons, the name can suggest a self enclosed by pride and precariously touched by human ties. The allusion remains compressed: the poem supplies neither a complete dramatic parallel nor a clear account of what this momentary revival achieves.
