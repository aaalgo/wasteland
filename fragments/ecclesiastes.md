# The Book of Ecclesiastes

Eliot's note directs the reader to Ecclesiastes 12:5, part of a poem about aging and death. Its images include fear of heights, a flourishing almond tree, a burdensome grasshopper, and the failure of desire, before the human being goes to an enduring home and mourners gather in the street.

“The cricket no relief” brings that passage into the dry landscape of “The Burial of the Dead.” The poem's dead tree, exposed stone, and failed shelter make a natural scene feel like a condition of exhausted life. The biblical background strengthens that atmosphere without turning every object into a fixed equivalent from Ecclesiastes.
