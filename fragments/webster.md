# John Webster's The White Devil

John Webster's *The White Devil* supplies the burial song Eliot recalls at the end of Part I. In Webster, the wolf is to be kept from the grave because it may dig up the body; Eliot changes the animal into the dog, supposedly humanity's friend. His notes also connect the wind at the door and the later image of the spider over memorials with Webster.

These scraps carry the Jacobean tragedy's atmosphere of murder, insecure burial, and mourning into modern conversation. Even friendship cannot guarantee the dead their rest. The later thought that people will quickly remarry turns private remembrance into another fragile defence against time.
