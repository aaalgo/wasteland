# The Road to Emmaus

In Luke 24:13–35, two disciples walk toward Emmaus after Jesus's death. The risen Jesus joins them, but they do not recognize him until he breaks bread; he then vanishes from their sight. Eliot identifies this journey as one of the themes used in the opening movement of Part V.

The unknown third walker recalls a presence that accompanies despair before it is understood. Eliot also connects the passage with an Antarctic expedition's sensed extra companion. Religious recognition and exhausted perception overlap, but the poem withholds the Gospel's decisive unveiling: its travellers keep asking who walks beside them.
