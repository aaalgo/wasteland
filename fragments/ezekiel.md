# The Book of Ezekiel

Eliot directs readers to Ezekiel 2:1 for “Son of man,” the address God uses to the prophet before commissioning him to speak. In its biblical setting the phrase marks a human listener confronted by a voice with authority, rather than someone confidently explaining the world on his own.

Eliot's address instead presses on the listener's inability to know what can grow from stony rubbish and broken images. The prophetic manner survives amid cultural debris. Later dry bones may also remind a reader of Ezekiel's vision of restored life in chapter 37, but Eliot's explicit note here identifies the address in chapter 2; resurrection remains a possible resonance, not a completed event in this passage.
