# St Magnus Martyr

St Magnus Martyr is the London church named amid the river streets and the noise of a nearby public bar. Eliot's note praises its interior as one of Wren's finest. “Ionian white and gold” concentrates the reader's attention on an unexpected architectural splendour inside the working city.

The church interrupts the passage's movement through gramophone music, commercial streets, and lunchtime chatter. Its beauty is called “inexplicable”: the poem records the encounter without turning it into an argument or a complete religious resolution. Within the surrounding fragments, a crafted space briefly holds together what the city otherwise scatters.
