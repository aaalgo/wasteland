# The Brihadaranyaka Upanishad

The thunder's three instructions come from the *Brihadaranyaka Upanishad*, in the story commonly numbered 5.2. Eliot's note gives 5.1 and translates the imperatives as giving, sympathizing, and controlling. In the story, gods, human beings, and demons hear the same syllable, “Da,” as the particular instruction each needs. A [translation with the Sanskrit passage](https://www.swami-krishnananda.org/brdup/brhad_V-02.html) sets out these different hearings.

Eliot rearranges the commands as Datta, Dayadhvam, Damyata, giving each a separate human scene. A single sound becomes several ethical demands. After so many quotations whose speakers cannot reach one another, thunder supplies a shared utterance that still requires each listener to interpret and act.
