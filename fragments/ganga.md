# The Ganges and the Monsoon

“Ganga” is the Ganges; “Himavant” names the Himalayan region from which its waters descend. After the European waste landscape and the empty chapel, the poem imagines a diminished river, limp leaves, distant clouds, and a jungle waiting in silence. The setting prepares the thunder's Sanskrit syllable.

Rain has been desired throughout Part V, but the thunder now brings an instruction as well as a possible change in weather. The waiting land connects bodily thirst to the need for giving, compassion, and self-restraint in the Upanishadic fable that follows. The poem does not describe a fully restored river: it holds the scene at the threshold of response.
