# Dante's Inferno

The crowd crossing London Bridge recalls *Inferno* III.55–57, where Dante is astonished by the multitude of the dead. The following sighs draw on *Inferno* IV.25–27, the description of Limbo. Eliot names both passages in his notes, bringing different regions of Dante's afterlife into a single morning commute.

Later, the turning key recalls *Inferno* XXXIII.46: Count Ugolino hears the tower locked while he and his children are imprisoned. Eliot places that irreversible sound beside Bradley's account of private experience. Across these returns, Dante's dead and captives give the modern city's routine and the isolated mind an audible, bodily form.
