# Verlaine's Parsifal

Eliot's note identifies Paul Verlaine's poem *Parsifal* as the source of the French line about children's voices singing in a dome. Verlaine's poem invokes the Grail world associated with Parsifal and brings its spiritual drama toward a musical, sacred conclusion.

Eliot places the line immediately after the song about Mrs Porter and her daughter washing their feet. A sacred musical space suddenly opens within the bawdy river passage. The juxtaposition can suggest purification or expose the distance between ritual promise and modern life; the poem leaves both tones audible.
