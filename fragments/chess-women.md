# Middleton's Women Beware Women

Eliot's note points to the chess game in Thomas Middleton's *Women Beware Women*, Act II, scene ii. Livia occupies Bianca's mother-in-law with a game while the Duke gains access to Bianca. The moves on the board accompany a manipulation of people and a sexual encounter structured by unequal power.

The allusion makes the proposed game in Eliot's drawing room uneasy. A routine pastime sits beside sleepless vigilance and an anticipated knock: domestic intimacy feels strategic rather than reassuring. The play's title also shadows the section's movement between women in very different circumstances, both exposed to pressures around sex, marriage, and dependence.
