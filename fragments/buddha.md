# The Buddha's Fire Sermon

The title of Part III refers to the Buddha's *Ādittapariyāya Sutta*, the Fire Sermon. It describes sense experience as burning with passion, hatred, and delusion, as well as the suffering of birth, aging, and death. Release requires disenchantment and dispassion toward what holds the senses captive. Eliot directs readers to Henry Clarke Warren's *Buddhism in Translations* in his note.

After the river scenes and the typist's encounter, “burning” becomes both diagnosis and refrain. Augustine's prayer breaks into the same passage. Eliot explicitly calls this meeting of eastern and western ascetic voices intentional; the poem brings their demands for release together without making their teachings identical.
