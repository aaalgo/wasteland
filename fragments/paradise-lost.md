# Milton's Paradise Lost

Eliot's note traces “sylvan scene” to Book IV of Milton's *Paradise Lost*. There the wooded prospect belongs to the landscape around Eden. In the London room, it becomes the supposed view behind an image of Philomela's rape and transformation.

A phrase associated with paradise frames an act of violation. The effect unsettles the room's cultivated beauty: mythology is displayed as decoration, but its suffering persists through the nightingale's voice. The allusion makes the distance between a lost garden and the luxurious interior audible in only two borrowed words.
