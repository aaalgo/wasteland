# Elizabeth I and the Earl of Leicester

Eliot's note cites J. A. Froude's history of Elizabeth I, which reproduces a report by the Spanish ambassador de Quadra to Philip II. On a river outing, Elizabeth and Lord Robert Dudley, later Earl of Leicester, joke about marriage in the ambassador's presence. This courtly scene lies behind the poem's royal pair in a gilded boat.

The remembered pageant interrupts the oily working Thames. Gold, bells, wind, and moving oars briefly supply a ceremonial past, but the Thames-daughters' accounts of sexual disappointment follow it. The sequence invites comparison between the glamour of royal intimacy and later river encounters without claiming the past was innocent or whole.
