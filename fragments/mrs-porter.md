# Mrs. Porter

Eliot says that the Mrs. Porter verses came to him from Sydney, Australia, and that he did not know the ballad's origin. His note establishes an orally transmitted popular song without providing a definitive account of its first singers or setting.

The moonlight and washing make a comic domestic picture just after the arrival of Sweeney has been announced through a transformed literary hunting scene. Immediately afterward, Verlaine's children sing beneath a cupola. These adjacent kinds of song let bodily washing brush against ritual purification without making Mrs. Porter herself into a securely identified religious symbol.
