# Phlebas the Phoenician Sailor

Madame Sosostris's drowned Phoenician Sailor returns as Phlebas in “Death by Water.” Eliot's note explicitly links the figures and lets them overlap with the merchant and Ferdinand from *The Tempest*. This is a network of recurring forms rather than a single character's continuous biography.

Phlebas has forgotten the commercial reckoning of profit and loss. The current strips his life down to bones and carries him through the stages of age and youth. The final address makes his death a warning to the reader, regardless of religious identity. Water, elsewhere desperately desired, offers no simple promise of restoration here.
