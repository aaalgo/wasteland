# John Day's The Parliament of Bees

Eliot cites John Day's *The Parliament of Bees* for the sound of horns and the arrival of Actaeon to see Diana in the spring. In the myth recalled by Day, Actaeon's encounter with the naked goddess leads to catastrophe rather than fulfilled possession.

Eliot replaces the mythic names with Sweeney and Mrs. Porter and adds motors to the horns. The old pursuit becomes a modern journey, poised between sexual comedy and menace. Together with the nearby borrowing from Marvell, the passage makes apparently casual city sounds carry a crowded memory of desire, pursuit, and death.
