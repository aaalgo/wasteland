# Madame Sosostris

Madame Sosostris combines the prestige of prophecy with a cold, appointments, and professional caution. A possible literary precursor is the disguised fortune-teller in Aldous Huxley's *Crome Yellow*, chapter XXVII: Mr Scogan performs as Sesostris, the Sorceress of Ecbatana. The [novel's scene](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Crome_Yellow/Chapter_XXVII) makes prophecy a theatrical social transaction.

Eliot's clairvoyante is comic, yet her cards anticipate the drowned Phoenician, the merchant, and the crowds that recur later. Her warning about water can be followed through the poem without treating her as an authoritative interpreter. The apparent fraud possesses fragments of knowledge whose connections she does not fully explain.
