# Saint Augustine's Confessions

“To Carthage then I came” recalls Book III of Saint Augustine's *Confessions*, where the young Augustine enters a city full of turbulent desires. Eliot's notes also identify the appeal “O Lord Thou pluckest me out” with the *Confessions*. The remembered city becomes both a historical place and a condition of the desiring self.

The prayers interrupt the Thames-daughters' broken accounts near the end of “The Fire Sermon.” Augustine's voice seeks rescue rather than another satisfaction. Eliot says that placing this Christian ascetic voice beside the Buddha's sermon is deliberate: the repeated “burning” joins traditions that diagnose desire differently but both imagine release from its captivity.
