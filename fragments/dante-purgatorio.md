# Dante's Purgatory

“Il miglior fabbro” means “the better craftsman.” It recalls *Purgatorio*
XXVI, 117, where Guido Guinizzelli points out the twelfth-century Provençal
troubadour Arnaut Daniel to Dante and praises him as the better craftsman
of his mother tongue, surpassing his rivals in vernacular poetry.
Eliot transfers this tribute to Ezra Pound in the dedication of *The Waste Land*.
See [Danteworlds, University of Texas at Austin](https://danteworlds.laits.utexas.edu/purgatory/09lust.html).

The phrase also returns Pound's own admiration for Daniel: chapter II of
his *The Spirit of Romance* (1910), devoted to Daniel, is titled
“Il miglior fabbro.” Pound continued this study in twelve articles on
Daniel published in 1911–12, which he intended to gather into a book.
See [the 1910 edition of *The Spirit of Romance*](https://archive.org/details/spiritofromancea00pounrich).

Daniel returns near the end of *The Waste Land* in “Poi s’ascose nel foco
che gli affina,” from *Purgatorio* XXVI, 148: he disappears into the fire
that purifies him. The dedication and this later fragment thus recall
the same canto and poet.
