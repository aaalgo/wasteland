# The Passion of Christ

The opening of Part V evokes Christ's Passion through torchlight, a garden, agony, arrest, and the juxtaposition of prison and palace. These details recall the Gospel accounts of Gethsemane and the events leading to the crucifixion. Eliot's note also names the journey to Emmaus as one of this section's governing themes.

The passage moves from one who has died to a collective “we” who are still dying. It borrows the shape of a sacred history without immediately supplying its promised resolution. The later companion on the road may reopen the possibility of recognition, but here the readers inhabit the interval of exhaustion, loss, and waiting.
