# Psalm 137: By the Rivers of Babylon

Psalm 137 begins with the exiles of Judah sitting beside the rivers of Babylon and weeping as they remember Zion. Asked by their captors for songs, they ask how they can sing the Lord's song in a foreign land. Water here carries displacement and the difficulty of making music after catastrophe.

Eliot replaces Babylon with Leman, the French name of Lake Geneva. The change puts a modern European place inside the biblical lament. Between the departed nymphs and Spenser's song to the Thames, this borrowed voice makes the riverbank a place where inherited songs survive as broken, displaced speech.
