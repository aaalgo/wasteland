# The Departed Nymphs

Nymphs are the spirits of the natural world in classical tradition, and river nymphs belong especially to the inherited language of pastoral poetry. In this passage, that tradition is immediately present through the borrowed refrain from Spenser's *Prothalamion*.

The announcement that the nymphs have departed also seems to refer to modern women who once met the sons of city directors by the Thames. Mythic beings and temporary lovers share a sentence, then disappear without forwarding addresses. The river loses both its poetic guardians and the litter of its summer pleasures: absence itself becomes the surviving evidence.
