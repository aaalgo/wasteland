# The Holy Grail and the Fisher King

The Fisher King belongs to the varied medieval Grail traditions. Eliot approaches them through Jessie L. Weston's *From Ritual to Romance*, which links the king's affliction and the land's barrenness with a quest for restoration. Eliot acknowledges this book as a source for his title, plan, and much incidental symbolism, and specifically points to Weston's Fisher King chapter for the final fishing scene.

Fishing behind a gashouse and fishing beside an arid plain bring the legendary role into diminished modern places. The empty chapel keeps the quest's architecture while withholding its sacred object. The whirlpool can be read within this network of water, ordeal, and possible renewal, but Eliot supplies no note making it an exclusive Grail symbol. At the end, the fisher asks whether he can order his lands; the poem leaves restoration as a question.
