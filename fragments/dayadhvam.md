# Dayadhvam: Sympathize

“Dayadhvam” means “be compassionate”; Eliot glosses it as “sympathize.” In the *Brihadaranyaka Upanishad* 5.2, it is the command the demons hear within Prajāpati's “Da.” The thunder repeats giving, self-restraint, and compassion for all to learn. See [the Upanishad's three commands](https://www.baps.org/Article/2011/The-Bruhadaranyaka-Upanishad-2024.aspx).

Eliot follows the word with a locked door, private prisons, and a briefly revived Coriolanus. The command is heard through evidence of its difficulty: each consciousness may repeat the thought of enclosure instead of reaching another. Dante and Bradley supply different kinds of prison, while the broken Shakespearean figure suggests a fragile possibility of contact.
