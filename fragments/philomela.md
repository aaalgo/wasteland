# Philomela and the Nightingale

Eliot points to Book VI of Ovid's *Metamorphoses*. Tereus rapes Philomela and cuts out her tongue to prevent her from telling what happened. She nevertheless communicates the crime through weaving. The ensuing violence ends in the transformation of the principal figures into birds.

The poem makes Philomela's nightingale song both an “inviolable voice” and a sound reduced to “Jug Jug” by unhearing listeners. Her return in “The Fire Sermon” breaks language into bird calls and the name of her attacker. Voice survives bodily violation, but survival does not guarantee that a listener will understand or honour what it says.
