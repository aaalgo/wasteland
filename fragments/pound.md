# Ezra Pound and the Better Craftsman

The dedication names Ezra Pound and calls him “il miglior fabbro,” the better craftsman. The phrase comes from Dante's *Purgatorio*, Canto XXVI, where it honours the poet Arnaut Daniel. Eliot thus places a contemporary literary friendship inside an inherited language of poetic judgment.

Pound's editorial work on the manuscript helped shape the published poem through substantial cuts and criticism. The [British Library's account of the annotated manuscript](https://www.britishlibrary.cn/en/works/the-waste-land/) documents that collaboration. For a poem built from other people's words, the dedication also acknowledges the other reader whose practical decisions helped determine how its fragments would meet.
