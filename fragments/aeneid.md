# Virgil's Aeneid

Eliot's note identifies *Aeneid* I.726–27 as the source of “laquearia,” the Latin word for a panelled or coffered ceiling. Virgil describes lamps hanging from the golden ceiling at Dido's banquet for Aeneas: the interior shines against the night outside.

In “A Game of Chess,” that epic hospitality survives as a word lodged in an oppressive modern room. Smoke troubles the ceiling's pattern, while jewels, perfumes, mirrors, and carved figures crowd the senses. Dido's world of splendid welcome and disastrous love offers a possible undertone; Eliot explicitly identifies the ceiling passage, rather than assigning his unnamed woman Dido's identity.
