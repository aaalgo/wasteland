# Wagner's Tristan and Isolde

Eliot's notes identify two quotations from Wagner's *Tristan und Isolde*. In Act I, a sailor's song calls toward an absent Irish girl across the sea. In Act III, the sea is described as desolate and empty while the dying Tristan waits for Isolde's arrival.

The German passages enclose the hyacinth-garden memory. An opening call of desire is answered by a sea emptied of the awaited figure. The garden's inability to speak therefore stands between musical voices of longing and absence, allowing romantic intensity and emotional failure to occupy the same remembered moment.
