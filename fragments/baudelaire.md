# Baudelaire and The Flowers of Evil

Eliot's note on “Unreal City” cites “Les Sept Vieillards” from Charles Baudelaire's *Les Fleurs du mal*: a crowded, dream-filled city where a ghost encounters a passer-by in daylight. London inherits the atmosphere of Baudelaire's Paris, a place in which ordinary streets and hallucination occupy the same space.

The end of “The Burial of the Dead” addresses the reader with the closing line of Baudelaire's prefatory poem “Au Lecteur”: the hypocritical reader is the speaker's likeness and brother. The accusation crosses the poem's frame. Readers cannot simply inspect the waste land from outside; the desires, evasions, and shared memories it assembles also belong to them.
