# The Tarot Cards

Eliot's note openly says that he departed from the Tarot pack to suit his purposes. Some cards belong to the traditional pack; others are invented or altered. He associates the Hanged Man with Frazer's hanged god and the hooded figure in Part V, and the man with three staves, arbitrarily, with the Fisher King.

The reading is therefore an arrangement of the poem's own returning images rather than a faithful lesson in divination. Sailor, merchant, wheel, and crowds prepare later passages. The cards offer a miniature model of reading fragments: their significance develops through recurrence, while the reader must hold together connections that no single speaker commands.
