# Margate Sands

The third of the Thames-daughters speaks from Margate Sands, outside the central London geography of the preceding voices. Her words repeat “nothing” until the inability to connect becomes both a personal condition and a problem for the poem's own assembly of fragments.

Broken fingernails and dirty hands bring the passage down from grand literary inheritance to bodily labour and deprivation. “My people” gestures toward a shared social history, but the speaker can offer no sustaining account of it. The poem leaves the relation between that exhausted community and its individual voice unresolved; the broken connection is part of what the fragment preserves.
